# My Journey Towards Software Purity

2025-12-07

"Everything should be running on OpenBSD. My desktop, router, web server, and even minor local services should *all* be built on top of the great Pufferfish BSD."

I told myself this "mantra" not too long ago, after diving head-first into the wonderful world of BSDs. The reasoning for this was to become well-versed in a single operating system, instead of only generally understanding a handful of systems at a top-level.  And in that time I managed to use OpenBSD as [my main workstation](), [my firewall / router](), and [my personal web server](). I had fulfilled my goal of achieving software purity (or what I *thought* was purity).

And it was pretty great - initially. Overtime small annoyances began to pop up. Mostly with my workstation. Certain applications were not available, my development workflow seemed slower than that on MacOS. Death by a thousand cuts, in a way. So I bailed on the original mantra.

Not long after, I was in a weird state where **none** of my hardware or services were using OpenBSD. Which felt almost *wrong*, as though I've betrayed some kind of sacred ideal. One that I imposed upon myself. Some services were still operating on open source software, but I felt like a sellout all the same.

After my departure from the world of OpenBSD, my setup was:

- MacOS for my personal workstation
- FreeBSD (indirectly, since it was shared hosting) for my web servers
- Linux / OpenWRT for my home router (based on lower power consumption)

And everything worked just *fine*. I was productive and the switchover didn't create any major hiccups with my workflows. 

But there was a nagging in the back of my mind. I missed the simplicity of doing things on OpenBSD. I missed the consistency across all services and devices. Maybe I didn't give OpenBSD a truly "fair shake". It seemed as though my "techie" subconscious just couldn't let it go...
